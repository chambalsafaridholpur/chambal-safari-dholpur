River Habitat

Sandbanks, water and ravines

Gharial

A distinctive, long-snouted crocodilian that feeds mainly on fish. Chambal is one of its most important remaining strongholds. It is critically endangered; observe only from a safe distance.

Mugger Crocodile

A broad-snouted freshwater crocodile also known as the marsh crocodile. It uses river edges, pools and wetlands. Never approach basking or swimming animals.

Gangetic River Dolphin

A freshwater dolphin adapted to life in the Ganges river system. It surfaces briefly to breathe, so sightings can be quick and are never guaranteed.

Indian Skimmer

A striking river bird with a long lower bill, often seen skimming the water surface to catch fish. Sandbars are important resting and nesting habitat.

Freshwater Turtles

The sanctuary supports several turtle species, including threatened species. They help maintain river ecosystems; nesting beaches and sandbanks should never be disturbed.

Smooth-coated Otter

A social, semi-aquatic mammal that hunts fish and other aquatic prey. It depends on healthy rivers and undisturbed banks.

Good to know

A few helpful answers.

Use these quick notes to prepare for a responsible visit.

Can I see gharials or crocodiles?

The Chambal region is known for its river reptiles, but wildlife sightings depend on season, conditions and chance. Keep a respectful distance and never approach animals.

Do I need to book in advance?

Availability and booking arrangements can vary. Contact your authorised local operator before travelling to confirm current options, costs and meeting points.

Are safari timings and prices listed here?

No. Timings, prices and services have not been verified for this page, so please confirm them directly with a local operator before making plans.

Is this page a booking service?

No. This is an informational website concept for Chambal Safari Dholpur. It does not collect payments or confirm reservations.
